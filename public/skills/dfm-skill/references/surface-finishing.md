# Surface Finishing

Use this reference when finish, coating, texture, cosmetics, corrosion resistance, friction, sealing, or post-processing affects manufacturability.

## Inputs To Capture

- Finish process: anodize, plating, passivation, powder coat, paint, bead blast, tumble, polish, vapor smooth, dye, texture, heat treat, black oxide, laser mark, or none.
- Finish purpose: corrosion resistance, wear, insulation, conductivity, cosmetic appearance, friction, cleanability, biocompatibility, UV stability, or sealing.
- Critical surfaces: cosmetic A/B/C faces, datums, sliding faces, threads, bores, seals, grounding points, bond areas, and masked areas.
- Timing: finish before or after machining, bending, welding, assembly, heat staking, insert installation, or inspection.
- Acceptance: color range, gloss, roughness, texture, thickness, masking, allowable rack marks, and witness marks.

## Dimensional Effects

- Coatings add, remove, or alter material. Account for buildup on holes, slots, threads, bores, pins, tabs, and sliding fits.
- Anodize and plating can change fit in small holes and threads; define whether dimensions apply before or after finish.
- Powder coat and paint can add substantial thickness, especially on edges and corners.
- Tumbling, blasting, polishing, and sanding can round edges and change small features.
- Heat treatment, stress relief, welding, and curing can distort parts before final inspection.

## Cosmetic Planning

- Mark A surfaces early so supports, gates, ejectors, tabs, pierce points, clamps, rack marks, and tool marks can be placed elsewhere.
- Define acceptable witness marks for molds, fixtures, racks, supports, tabs, and tooling.
- Avoid placing critical cosmetic surfaces where support removal, deburring, weld cleanup, or gate trimming is unavoidable.
- Add radii and avoid sharp burr-prone edges on visible or handled parts.
- For textured molded parts, increase draft and clarify where texture stops at parting lines or shutoffs.

## Masking And Keep-Outs

- Mask threads, bearing seats, seal grooves, electrical contacts, grounding pads, bonding surfaces, precision datums, and press-fit areas when finish would hurt function.
- Make mask boundaries easy to fixture and inspect; avoid tiny irregular masks unless necessary.
- Add drainage and hanging/racking options for wet processes where trapped solution is a risk.
- Avoid blind cavities that trap plating solution, abrasive, resin, dye, paint, or cleaning fluid.

## Process Notes

- Anodize: check alloy compatibility, color variation, Type II versus Type III thickness, electrical contact marks, and fatigue impact for hardcoat.
- Plating: plan for buildup, hydrogen embrittlement risk in high-strength steels, masking, rack marks, and post-bake requirements.
- Powder coat: account for edge buildup, orange peel, masking, cure temperature, and interference with tight assemblies.
- Paint: define surface prep, primer, color, gloss, masking, cure, and allowable defects.
- Bead blast and tumble: useful for uniform matte finishes and deburring, but can soften details and alter small edges.
- Polishing: expensive on complex geometry, inside corners, and deep pockets; specify only where needed.
- Passivation: improves stainless corrosion behavior but does not remove heavy scale, burrs, or contamination by itself.
- Vapor smoothing and resin finishing: can improve printed surface quality but may soften edges or change fits.

## Common Failure Modes

- Coating closes a clearance hole, slot, thread, snap feature, or sliding gap.
- Masking misses a datum, ground contact, or bonding surface.
- Cosmetic face receives support scars, rack marks, gate blush, ejector marks, weld cleanup, or pierce marks.
- Sharp edges chip paint or powder coat.
- Trapped fluid causes corrosion, contamination, outgassing, or delayed leaks.
- Polished or blasted datums no longer meet tolerance after finishing.
- Color or gloss varies between materials, batches, suppliers, or wall thicknesses.

## Recommended Actions

- State finish thickness assumptions and whether dimensions are before or after finish.
- Add masking notes and keep-out zones for functional surfaces.
- Move supports, gates, tabs, racks, or pierces away from cosmetic and sealing faces.
- Increase clearances for coated assemblies.
- Add drain holes, vent holes, or fixture holes where finishing requires them.
- Use sample chips, boundary samples, or first articles for critical cosmetics.
- Keep finish callouts local when only a few faces need tight cosmetic or roughness requirements.
